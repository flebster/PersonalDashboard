For more than 15 years, we’ve scored products to help people understand what makes them repairable. But we’re a small team, and there are lots of products in the world; we’ve come to accept that we can’t score them all.

Several jurisdictions have been working on repairability scoring efforts: France kicked things off in 2021, and the Joint Research Centre followed. This month, repairability scores for smartphones have been required at point of sale for a full year in Europe.

Just last month, the New York legislature passed the first repairability labeling law in the US and awaits gubernatorial action. But there’s not yet a standard that describes how to create scorecards for the US context. We’re hoping to change that.

That’s why we’re teaming up with a standard development organization, the National Sanitation Foundation, to develop a voluntary US repairability scoring standard, distinct from iFixit’s existing scores. Our three-phase approach:

Phase 1: We will adapt the EU/JRC smartphone methodology for the US context.

Phase 2: We will create a framework for creating future product scorecards, implementing international scoring standards EN 45554 and the developing IEC 63683-2 (to which we will continue to contribute).

Phase 3: We will convene experts to develop additional scorecards.

The iFixit/NSF standard will not itself be regulation, but it should provide a credible reference for policymakers, purchasers, researchers, and industry.

We’re seeking manufacturers, repairers, researchers, consumer advocates, sustainability experts, validators, and other stakeholders to join the technical committee. Technical committee and observer applications are available here, as well as full policies and a draft timeline. Applications are due July 13 to Emily Markwell (emarkwell@nsf.org).

About NSF
NSF is an independent, global services organization dedicated to improving human health for more than 80 years by developing public health standards and providing world-class testing, inspection, certification, advisory services and digital solutions to the food, water, and wellness products industries.  Accredited by the American National Standards Institute (ANSI) and the Standards Council of Canada (SCC), NSF develops standards through a public process that ensures balanced input from industry representatives, government, academia and NGOs, sustainability representatives, and users. It provides expertise in governance, transparency, and stakeholder engagement for iFixit’s criteria development.
