    • The idea of a replaceable-blade knife isn’t new. If you’ve ever used a utility knife—otherwise known as a box cutter—you’re familiar with the concept. Instead of the permanent solid blade in the design of most pocket knives, a replaceable-blade knife features swappable, disposable blade inserts that go in when the old ones get dull. The convenience of quickly getting a factory-sharp razor edge is invaluable when speed matters. Plus, they don’t need to be sharpened—a task that can be tedious and hard to perfect.

    These knives are also versatile. Whereas a traditional box cutter is limited to a few blade shapes, knife systems with proprietary blades offer more options. And if you’re looking at cost, replaceable-blade knives are often priced lower than pocket knives because cheaper steel can be used, which makes replacement blades affordable as well.

    For everyday tasks or prying jobs where you need a strong blade and point, a regular pocket knife is likely the better choice. But for who doesn’t have the time, tools, or skills to sharpen regular knives, a replaceable-blade knife may be the answer. We tested some of the top models aimed at tradespeople, hunters, and EDC folks, and share our favorites below.

    Outdoor Edge has made its name producing knives tailored to the specific game-processing needs of hunters. But as the brand developed its lineup of replaceable-blade knives, it managed to build what I consider the best all-around, do-everything replaceable-blade knife: the Outdoor Edge Razor VX5.

    One reason the Razor VX5 stands out is the ecosystem of blades behind it, which includes a range of blade styles—from all-around drop-point to gut hooks to straight-edge utility and even new fillet blades. This versatility means it can adapt to different scenarios while keeping the same familiar folding knife in your pocket.

    The knife itself is just a well-made flipper action folding pocket knife. Ceramic bearings give it a smooth action that’s easy to deploy and close one-handed, with a secure liner lock to keep the knife rigid and safe when in use. The blade release is also much less fiddly than your standard utility blade retention, making it fast and easy to swap. It’s a low-profile button that requires some intention to release the blade so the mechanism stays simple, avoiding screws and other special locks that bulk other replaceable-blade knives.

    The aesthetic on many utility knives can be utilitarian, but the Razor VX5 actually looks cool with its black carbon-fiber and G10 scales and a modern EDC folder shape. The scales are fairly thick, adding a bit of weight but also rigidity for a more robust feel. One drawback to the handle is the lack of texture that can challenge your grip in cold or wet conditions.

    If you’re shopping for a dedicated task-specific utility blade or a hunting season skinner, you might find one of the other options below better suited to your task. But if you’re looking for an everyday replaceable-blade knife that can adapt to different tasks easily, it’s hard to top the VX5.

    Like the Outdoor Edge VX5 recommended above, the Coast 1919 Reserve Shift improves on the basic utility knife design by offering three included blade shapes and a more modern pocket knife design. It just does it for less and with a little less style.

    The 1919 Reserve Shift uses proprietary blades from Coast, but has the advantage of being compatible with standard two-notch utility blades. The knife ships with one each of a drop-point blade, a gut hook, and a standard two-notch utility blade.

    The 1919 Reserve Shift also comes with a belt sheath that stores extra blades. I like to pocket carry it using the pocket clip, but the sheath is nice to have to keep everything together in a pack or if you like to belt carry. I prefer designs that can store extra blades on or in the knife itself, but something is better than nothing to keep the extras organized.

    While it offers a great value, the design is definitely function before form. The plastic and rubber handle scales provide great grip but feel disposable compared with most modern pocket knives. The design uses the thumb stud as a blade locking mechanism, which I found unintuitive and potentially prone to accidentally releasing the blade.

    Still, for the price, it’s an effective knife that offers versatility and exchangeable-blade convenience in a pocket knife form.

    What to Look For

    Blade System

    One unique difference with replaceable-blade systems is that some use proprietary blades and others use the familiar two-notch straight-edge razor blades. If you choose a knife that can only use the latter, you have the advantage of widely available, cheap blades, but the downside is that you’re limited in potential blade shapes. Proprietary blade systems tend to have more blades but cost more. Which is right for you depends on how you plan to use the blade.

    Carry Options

    Unlike standard pocket knives, replaceable-blade knives aren’t necessarily going to be pocket-carried. Some are fixed blades, and some don’t even come with a pocket clip and are meant for being carried in your pack rather than on your person. If you’re looking at a replaceable-blade knife for everyday carry, you’ll want to make sure it has a pocket clip. If you’re considering one of the hunting-focused knives, you may not care about carry options, but think about if you prefer a fixed blade or folding pocket knife style.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    Each knife is different with many uses brand-specific proprietary blades and others employing the familiar two-notch box cutter-style razor blades. (You can find utility hook blades that use the two-notch platform as well.) If you’re trying to keep costs down and don’t mind always using a straight edge, look for a knife that accepts the two-notch blades. We highlight the type of blade used for each of the recommendations above.

    If you want a lot of blade style options for you replaceable blade knife, Outdoor Edge offers the widest variety of blade sizes and shapes with traditional pocket knife blade styles such as drop point, sheepsfoot, tanto plus utility and specialized blades. We recommend two different Outdoor Edge options above. The Coast Reserve 1919 Shift option we recommend above has the ability to use both their proprietary blade shapes as well as standard utility blades.

    A bit of blade play or movement is normal for many utility knives and replaceable blade systems. Some knives, like the Civivi Elementum above, minimize this movement with set screws or other locking mechanisms if that’s a concern. One downside to these measures is they often make it more tedious to swap blades so consider the trade-off before you buy.

    Each replaceable blade knife is different, with many being aimed at specialized tasks such as construction jobs or dressing wild game. In general, replaceable blade and utility knives offer the convenience of quickly swapping in fresh, sharp blades instead of needing to sharpen your knife. The straight-edge, razor utility blades are especially good for slicing and scoring tough materials from cordage to plastic and slicing through cardboard and even tough leather.
