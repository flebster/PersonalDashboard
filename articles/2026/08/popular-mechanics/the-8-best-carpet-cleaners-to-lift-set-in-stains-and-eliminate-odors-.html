    • Vacuums are great for everyday cleaning, but if you need something to go the extra mile, a carpet cleaner gets deep into fibers to lift dirt, pet hair, and tough stains through a mix of scrubbing power, suction, and cleaning solution. This freshens up the look of your carpet beyond what a regular vacuuming is capable of, and extends its life by removing debris that damages the fibers if left untreated.

    While it’s possible to rent heavy-duty carpet cleaning machines from home improvement stores, in the long run it’s more convenient and economical to purchase your own and use it as needed. We typically recommend them for spot treatment of spills and stains, as well as for a deep clean of your carpets once every couple months depending on foot traffic. Most carpet cleaners are corded uprights (though there are a few canister-style models) and they typically include attachments for dealing with specific messes, like mud or pet stains.

    Our favorite models come from trusted brands like Bissell and Hoover—and many cost roughly the same as regular vacuums. These are the best carpet cleaners we recommend, along with advice on how to pick the right model for your home and needs.

    What to Consider Before Buying

    Get the right size model for the job.

    Commercial-grade carpet cleaners feature the largest water tanks and the most powerful scrubbing or steaming features to dislodge dirt and remove stains. This allows them to cover much larger areas of carpet at a time before they require a refill with water or cleaning solution.

    Similarly, because they are commercial grade, most of them last for years and rarely need a repair. The tradeoff, of course, is that they aren’t easy to store and cost more than smaller models do. For the most part, it's got more power and capacity than you'll actually need.

    For most households, we recommend a full-size carpet cleaner. They take up about as much space as a standard upright vacuum and come with a gallon-size tank that allows them to run for approximately 15 to 20 minutes. This is typically enough to cover small rooms in one tank and larger rooms with a single refill.

    If you have small rugs or sections of carpeting, a portable carpet cleaner is the way to go. They’re generally no bigger than a handheld vacuum and feature an attached hose and a handful of cleaning tools. These are best for spot cleaning and small stains on furniture items. Many full-size models come with attached hoses for spot treatment as well.

    Choose a model with powerful cleaning features.

    Most carpet cleaners work by shooting a mix of water and cleaning solution at the carpet and then vacuuming it back up once it removes the set-in dirt and grime. This baseline design is typically effective for pulling up moderate dirt and light stains, but it may not be enough for larger issues.

    Many of the carpet cleaners we recommend offer more than just water and suction. Several full-size models have built-in roller brushes that scrub the carpet to dislodge dirt in tandem with the cleaning spray and suction functions.

    Some models, like the Bissell Revolution HydroSteam, clean using heated water or steam to more effectively break down dirt and set-in grime, while others use hot air to dry carpets after a wash, which is particularly helpful in homes with kids and pets where you won't have the time to let the carpet dry naturally.

    Bonus attachments and tools add value.

    Some full-size models, like the Hoover PowerDash Pet Compact Carpet Cleaner, often come with special hoses and heads for upholstery cleaning or spot treatment on stains. Others come with tools designed for digging into carpets and lifting away pet hair and dander. These have scrubbing brushes on the end that pull up dander and hair more easily than models with traditional suction designs.

    FAQs

    What cleaning solution should I buy?

    Most carpet cleaners on this list will provide sample cleaning solutions from their brand which you can try out for yourself before committing to any one individual option. For the most part, the specialized options built for specific stains likely won't make a huge difference in day to day cleaning in comparison to generic ones. Concentrated formulas are well suited for lifting up serious dirt and stains similar to your laundry detergent, but may not be necessary for light refreshes in low foot traffic areas.

    In fact, many people use vinegar solutions or substitute with a mix of laundry detergent, fabric softener, and water. While we don't necessarily recommend that, these homemade options should work fine in an pinch and will still be effective in cleaning your carpet.

    Should you get an upright cleaner or portable option?

    Most households will find that an upright cleaner will provide the best balance of power and convenience, since these machines deep clean entire rooms efficiently.

    “Upright is great if you’re cleaning whole rooms with wall to wall carpet,” says Ryan Knoll, owner of Tidy Casa, a professional cleaning company that services more than 600 homes a month. He also owned a carpet cleaning company for many years, learning firsthand (and often the hard way) what works and what doesn’t when it comes to upright versus portable carpet cleaners.

    However, Knoll says portable carpet cleaners have rightfully earned their spot as well. “Portable carpet cleaners are great for spot cleaning and getting into smaller spaces,” he explains. “Think, pet accidents, upholstery, or cars.”

    Can you use a carpet cleaner on other types of surfaces like tile or hardwood?

    Some carpet cleaners include attachments designed for upholstery, car interiors, and stairs. These tools are great for cleansing fabric surfaces around the house, but most experts advise against using carpet cleaners on hardwood or tile floors since excess moisture can damage those materials.

    “These carpet cleaners do one thing really well: they push moisture through the carpet and pull it back out,” Knoll says. “This is what catches all the muck and gets that deep clean. With that said, wood floors don't like moisture, so this is a no-fly zone for wood. Tile is the same.” Instead, Knoll recommends using a steam mop for tile or finished cement surfaces.

    How often should you deep clean a carpet?

    Cleaning professionals generally recommend deep-cleaning carpets once or twice a year. “For most homes, every 6 to 12 months will keep things super fresh,” Knoll says. “This works great for homes with 2 to 4 people, maybe a pet or two. For busier homes with lots of kids and pets, stepping it up to 3 to 4 months helps.”
