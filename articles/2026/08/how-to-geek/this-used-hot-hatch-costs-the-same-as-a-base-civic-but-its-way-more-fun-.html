A lightly used 2023 Golf GTI undercuts new economy cars

Practical, comfortable, and tons of fun

New cars are getting ridiculously pricey, and even a cheap run-around will set you back quite a lot. In 2026, there are no longer any new cars out there for under $20,000, and even traditionally cheap options are slowly pushing over the $25,000-mark. Instead of spending your money on something basic and dull, the 2023 Volkswagen Golf GTI delivers tons of fun at essentially the same price.

Even when new, the 2023 GTI wasn't the most expensive hatchback out there, with a starting price of between $31,625 and $40,965, depending on which trim level you opted for. Just a couple of years later, you can pick them up on the used market and save yourself a good chunk of money. With the base trim listing for an average price of just over $25,000, it's not much more expensive than a base Honda Civic.

We think that the GTI doesn't need any gimmicky tech features or fancy luxuries to be good. What makes the little hot hatch so good is that it keeps things simple. To this end, we would opt for either the S or the 40th Anniversary Edition model. The S comes really well-equipped for an entry level model, offering things like heated sports seats, automatic climate control, and a limited slip differential. If you're willing to spend a little extra, the 40th Anniversary model gets DCC adaptive chassis control and came standard with summer performance tires.

The GTI is a fun little car that is easy to live with

Entertaining but uncompromised

For the price that you pay, the Volkswagen Golf GTI offers an impressively refined and well-balanced driving experience. Not only is it an absolute joy to drive along twisty back roads, but it is compliant and comfortable enough that it never feels out of place on the highway or on city streets.

Playful without ever being overwhelming

Every 2023 Golf GTI comes with the same setup. Under the hood sits a perky turbocharged four-cylinder. Compared to the previous generation model, it makes an extra 13 horses and 15 pound-feet of torque. Power is sent exclusively to the front wheels. A six-speed manual is standard kit, but there are plenty of examples on the used market that come equipped with the dual-clutch transmission instead. As good as the DCT is, we think this car deserves a manual.

The Golf GTI is an incredibly approachable performance car. It is always eager to be pushed, but never feels daunting. It comes with a lot of standard performance-oriented kit that helps make it such a fun and engaging experience, with every model coming fitted with an electronic limited-slip differential and brake-based torque vectoring. It's comfortable too, with the German hatch's ride being compliant enough for you to drive it every day.

It might be a little basic, but it comes with all the necessities

The GTI is practical and modern

For a hot hatch to be truly successful, we think that it should be practical enough to be used as your daily driver. While it isn't exactly cavernous on the inside, we think that Volkswagen nails this with the GTI. It might not offer the most luxurious interior, but it comes with all the modern tech features that you could hope for.

Every GTI is reasonably practical, with plenty of room up front for even taller drivers and a back seat that can occasionally fit adults, as long as the journey isn't too long. The neat dashboard and center console layout up front also makes it feel bigger than it is. Cargo space is pretty reasonable for a car at this price point, with more than enough room in the trunk for a couple of weekend bags.

The design in the GTI is modern and fairly minimalist. While there are some red aesthetic flares and GTI badges, things on the inside are kept relatively simple. The build quality and materials used on the inside are a little low-rent, but no worse than any new compact car at this price. Top trims come with some fancy features, including a panoramic sunroof and leather upholstery, but we find the GTI more charming in its standard form.

Infotainment and technology

This is one of the rare cases where we think that the infotainment setup in the base model is actually better than what you get in higher trims. An 8.25-inch infotainment screen is standard, featuring a few physical controls, including a volume knob. Higher trims do come with a larger 10-inch screen, but they require you to use awful touch-sensitive sliders instead.

While it doesn't come with any cutting-edge tech features that will blow you away, the GTI comes with all the modern necessities. Every model comes with Apple CarPlay and Android Auto capability, though the base model requires you to connect via a cable. Adaptive cruise control is also standard. Top trims come with things like a Harman/Kardon sound system and a heads-up display.

Instead of that dull base economy car, the GTI offers some strong value

Excitement and fun are becoming harder and harder to come buy in the automotive world, especially if you're not looking to spend ludicrous amounts of money. If your aim is to spend less than $30,000, your options are pretty limited. However, if you aren't afraid of a few miles on the odometer, then a used 2023 GTI is a hard deal to pass up. Its affordable, fun, and practical, and a very easy car to live with day-to-day.
