If you've fired up HBO Max in the past few days, you've undoubtedly noticed the spotlight firmly focused on its new DCU series, Lanterns, and, if you're really caught up, the new season of Conan O'Brien Must Go—both of which we wrote about earlier this week. 3 ER: Caught on Camera (Season 1)

Wild and terrifying emergencies, filmed by the patients themselves

HBO may be the home of prestige TV, but when it has its wild cousin from out of town, TLC, over, you know the stories are going to be off the chain. The often-salacious reality-centric channel (lovingly referred to as "Terrible Life Choices") has been mining hospital drama for decades, but its latest series hands the cameras over to patients instead.

ER: Caught on Camera rolled out on TLC on Wednesday, with each of its eight weekly episodes hitting HBO Max the next day. The voyeuristic series pairs some gobsmacking self-shot footage of real ER emergencies with sit-down interviews and stories from the people they happened to. Real ER doctors and experts also help break down the cases and treatments, as the patients relive them. While some are lighter and less intense, be warned: some are harrowing close calls and can be tough to watch.

Everything and anything can happen, and it runs the gamut—everything from cardiac arrests, botched lip augmentations, and massive bee swarms to a woman with a rare split uterus and an animal trainer in Utah who gets death-rolled by an alligator during a live feeding. Buckle up.

1 Roadrunner: A Film About Anthony Bourdain

A loving and raw portrait of the beloved nomad chef

It's been more than eight years since the death of TV's most beloved nomadic chef and travel guru, Anthony Bourdain, and we're still feeling it. With the highly anticipated wide theatrical release of Matt Johnson's new biopic, Tony, this weekend, I found a perfect appetizer streaming on HBO Max—Morgan Neville's (20 Feet from Stardom) gorgeous documentary, Roadrunner: A Film About Anthony Bourdain.

An Oscar-winning director, Neville uses a mountain of beautiful and intimate archival footage, unaired outtakes from his shows, and more, as he traces Bourdain's unlikely rise from a little-known New York restaurant through his Kitchen Confidential stardom and his evolution into a world-travelling TV host.

Bourdain's own voice from the footage narrates much of his journey, but there's also plenty of insightful and touching interview time with subjects like chefs Éric Ripert and David Chang, artist David Choe, and most importantly, Bourdain's second wife, Ottavia Busia. The 91%-rated doc is a must-see for fans of the charismatic Bourdain.
