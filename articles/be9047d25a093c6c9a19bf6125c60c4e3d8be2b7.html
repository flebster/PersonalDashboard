The best home gym basics (under $100)

The name of the game in home fitness is space-saving fundamentals. If your gift recipient is new to at-home workouts, these options are a great way to help them get started on this fitness journey.

A solid set of resistance bands may not be flashy, but it’s one of the highest bang-for-your-buck gifts you can give. There are plenty of options to choose from for around $25 typically. (You could also spring for the popular TRX resistance band set for $179.95.)

Another must-have is a kettlebell or two. For a beginner, that might be a 25-pound kettlebell, which shouldn't cost more than $40 (if your gift recipient is stronger, obviously go heavier). The basic cast-iron type is usually the cheapest, while colorful competition-style kettlebells feel a bit nicer. If you're feeling extra fancy, the BowFlex SelectTech 840 Adjustable Kettlebell (available for $150) lets you easily adjust from 8 to 40 pounds with a turn of the dial.

Pair some basic resistance bands with a kettlebell—plus an ab-roller like this one for $23.99—and you'll have covered strength, conditioning, and core work for under $100 total.

The best gifts for strength training ($100–$400)

A doorway pull-up bar is the classic pick here. This one is available for $27.99 and barely takes up any space, but still, it opens up a ton of possibilities. However, make sure your gift recipient’s doorframe allows you to wedge in a doorframe bar; otherwise, you might try a pull-up tower like this one for $89.99.

If you ask me, pull-up bars tend to be overrated (and underused in practice). That's why my top recommendation for gift-giving here has to be adjustable dumbbells. I believe they’re the single best gift in this range for anyone serious about strength training at home.

Instead of a rack of a dozen dumbbell pairs, one set dials from roughly five to over 50 pounds per hand, all while storing away in a space the size of a shoebox. If budget allows, this could be the gift that actually allows someone to start lifting at home.

Prices vary greatly in this category, ranging from true budget options under $100, to my favorite Nuobells at $795. Your safest bet is probably the PowerBlock Elite EXP Adjustable Dumbbells, typically available for around $399.99.

The best big-ticket home gym equipment ($300-1,000+)

This gift territory might be for a "the whole family chips in" situation. At-home cardio is a beautiful gift, but most reliable cardio machines start creeping near the $1,000 mark. Still, I know of solid budget options before we get there.

What do you think so far?

For cardio without a treadmill-sized commitment (or price), a stationary bike is a great gift that can be found for under $300. Similarly, walking pads are a popular option, especially if your gift recipient works from home. I recommend the Merach W50 TrekPad for $329.99, primarily for its incline range (up to 12%).

If your budget allows, a Peloton is quite the gift. The new Cross-Training line launched in the fall of 2025, so it’s still pretty new:

  • The Cross-Training Bike has the same size of screen and the same main features as the original Bike, but adds a swivel mount to the screen, a more comfortable seat, and upgraded internals: better quality audio, a faster processor, and better wifi and Bluetooth connections. It’s normally $1,695. but frequently goes on sale.

  • The Cross-Training Bike+ has similarly upgraded internals compared to the regular Bike+. It adds a movement-tracking camera for AI-based form coaching in strength workouts, and can take voice commands. It also has a fan and a phone tray. (It has a swivel mount, too, but that’s not new for the Bike+.) It’s normally $2,695, but similarly goes on sale fairly regularly.

  • The Cross-Training Tread (aka treadmill), like the Cross-Training Bike, adds a swivel mount and upgraded internals. It’s normally $3,295.

For more hybrid athletes or strength-focused gift recipients, all-in-one functional trainers are a smart option—especially smart home gym systems. Think compact, wall-mounted or freestanding units with digital resistance and guided programming built-in. These are a trendy option for someone who wants structure and coaching baked into their workouts, rather than figuring out how to program them themselves, though popular options like Tonal and Speediance typically run over $3,000. This would be a mighty generous gift, but I'd be remiss not to mention them as an option.

A few things worth knowing before you complete your checkout, so that your gift really shines:

  • Prioritize materials like steel and metal over plastic wherever possible.

  • I'd avoid trendy items you found onTikTok, but if you do want something unconventional, read my guide here to make sure you're not getting scammed.

  • Make sure your gift recipient not only has the floor space, but the storage space for your gift.
