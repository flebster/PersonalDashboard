When reviewing riding or zero-turn mowers, I often call it out when they have a fabricated deck. Fabricated decks are one of the hallmarks of a higher-quality, more expensive mower. Does this mean they’re better for you, though? Generally, a fabricated deck is more durable, but that may not mean it’s an absolute must-have feature for you. So, the answer is: it depends.

Fabricated Decks Cost More to Make

Budget is frequently an important consideration when shopping for a new zero-turn or traditional riding mower. You’ll see an obvious increase in price between a mower built with a stamped deck and one with a fabricated deck. It costs more to manufacture a fabricated deck because it uses more steel and there are more steps involved in the process, as multiple pieces are welded together. A stamped deck is generally made from one piece of steel that’s pressed into shape in a single step. When cost is a deciding factor, stamped decks win.

Stamped Decks May Offer Better Cut Quality

Performance can mean different things to different folks. For the sake of this discussion, I’m focusing on cut quality and deck discharge force. Durability is something I would include with performance, but I’ll talk about that more specifically below.

The shape of this John Deere zero-turn mower is typical for a stamped deck.

Stamped mower decks allow smoother internal shapes and make it easier to manage airflow. The deck can be tightly formed around the top and circumference of the blade paths, which allows for smooth transitions between the blades and better airflow, which in turn allows more debris to pass out the discharge chute. This is especially important when using a bagging attachment, and you need to send all of the debris to the bag at the back of the mower. In this respect, stamped decks are often better.

However, that tight clearance between the stamped deck and the blades can also cause clogging with heavy, wet grass and reduce airflow. For this reason, stamped decks require more frequent cleaning. Over time, all mower decks will collect grass that sticks to the inside, but fabricated decks are less susceptible to reduced performance because of grass clogs.

Regarding cut quality, stamped decks can offer higher cut quality. This is due to the higher air velocity and the suction they create, pulling the grass blades upward and cutting them more evenly. However, the bigger the deck, the more difficult it can be to manage cut quality—and bigger decks, over 54 inches wide, are typically going to be fabricated.

It is important to note that cut quality between various mowers with stamped decks can vary widely, so it is a good idea to consult reviews and consider well-known brands—or demo a mower, if possible. Regardless of which type of deck you have, if you’re concerned about cut quality, one of the most important factors is blade selection. Using high-lift blades, for example, can help increase velocity and suction under the deck—as well as keeping them sharp, of course.

Fabricated Decks Offer Greater Durability

This is where the difference between fabricated and stamped decks becomes more significant. Fabricated decks are made from heavier-gauge steel and, in most cases, will last longer—from their potential lifespan to their ability to withstand abuse.

Whether it’s wet or not, there is still water inside the blades of grass. Any time a mower is used, the underside of the deck is subjected to this moisture, causing some grass to stick to the inside of the deck. If it’s not cleaned regularly, layers of grass build up over time and take longer and longer to dry out.

If you store your mower in a shed that isn’t climate-controlled, the debris stuck to the deck is constantly absorbing moisture. Stamped decks, with their thinner steel, are more susceptible to rusting through from this moisture. Of course, you should regularly clean out your deck regardless of the deck type, but it’s more critical on a stamped deck.

Stamped decks usually have a rolled edge to help make them stiffer. Some models may also have a bar welded on the outside edge to protect them from damage.

In the course of using a mower, you’ll eventually be following an edge or mowing around a tree, post, or other obstacle. Despite your best efforts, bumping into something is inevitable. Stamped decks usually have a rolled edge to stiffen them up, but it doesn’t necessarily take many bumps before that edge is folded up or dented. And a good enough snag on an immovable object can potentially twist the whole deck. The chance of this grows with wider decks—48 inches and up—because they stick out farther beyond the wheels on each side.

Fabricated decks are made from thicker steel and usually have an extra layer of steel welded around the edges. This helps them withstand harder impacts.

Fabricated decks, aside from being made from thicker steel, often have a welded strip around the bottom edge, effectively doubling their thickness. The combination of thicker steel and reinforcement makes this type of deck better able to withstand abuse. And, in the event they do rust through or somehow break, they are easier to weld and repair due to their thicker construction at baseline.

So, Which Type Do You Need?

If you have a large area to maintain and need a big mower to do so—say, one to five acres—and you plan on keeping the mower for a long time, I would opt for a more expensive mower with a fabricated deck. The investment will be worth it. But if you have a smaller yard and only want a 42- to 48-inch deck, then a mower with a stamped deck might be a good choice. Another dividing line might be between a smooth, golf course–like lawn versus a rougher and more primitive lawn; consider a stamped deck for the former, and a fabricated deck for the latter.
