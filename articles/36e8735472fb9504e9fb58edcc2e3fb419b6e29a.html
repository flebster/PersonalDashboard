An Easy Pita Bread for Beginners

Pita is a traditional, round, leavened flatbread often served throughout the Middle East and Mediterranean. This recipe here is my easy at-home version, and it’s perfect for beginners (I first shared this homemade pita bread recipe way back in 2010, and the simple method I use STILL stands!)

The dough for this one is a little different from my homemade naan, with warm water, yeast, sugar, olive oil, whole wheat flour, all-purpose flour, and salt, but the execution is almost exactly the same. The biggest difference is that these pitas bake in a super-hot 500ºF oven instead of in a skillet. That intense heat quickly turns the moisture in the dough into steam, causing each evenly rolled round to puff up and separate into two soft, tender layers. I remove them once they’re fully inflated but still pale so they stay soft and lightly chewy rather than becoming dry and crisp. The small amount of whole wheat flour gives them a slightly nuttier flavor and a little extra color, but you can replace it with more all-purpose flour if that’s what you have!

  1. Add the flour gradually. I usually use around 3 cups total, but humidity, elevation, and the way flour is measured can all affect how much you need. Stop when the dough feels soft and pliable but no longer sticky. I prefer to go by how the dough looks and feels, so I highly recommend checking the step-by-step photos below for reference before you begin!
  2. Don’t rush the rise. There’s only one rise, and it usually takes about an hour, but the dough should double in size before you continue. This gives it time to develop the airiness and elasticity it needs to bake up soft rather than dense. If it’s taking a while, place the covered bowl in a turned-off oven with the light on or on a heating pad set to low (if you’ve got one).
  3. Roll the dough thinly and evenly. I roll each piece into a 6-inch circle, which brings it to the right thickness. Your pita will NOT puff up if it’s too thick. Uneven spots can also stop the pocket from forming properly in the oven, so make sure each round is an even thickness from the center to the edges.
  4. Let the oven get really hot. Pita needs an immediate blast of heat to set the outside of the dough as the moisture inside turns to steam and puffs it up. Because this method doesn’t use a hot skillet, stone, or steel, I always let the oven fully preheat to 500ºF before baking.
  5. Keep the rolled rounds covered. Place a damp cloth over the dough while it rests as the oven preheats so the surface and edges don’t dry out or crack.

Pita bread is one of those magical ingredients you can use in many ways. Tear it into wedges for dipping, crisp it up in the oven, stuff the pockets with fillings, or use a whole pita as the base for an open-faced sandwich situation. One of my favorite ways to serve it is torn into wedges and dipped straight into my homemade hummus. You can then turn that into a simple mezze-style spread with olives, fresh veggies, and anything else you have on hand!

For a crunchier texture, brush the pita with olive oil and bake it into chips instead. I’ve even tossed them with cinnamon sugar in the past to make cinnamon pita crisps for a sweet snack, and they’re absolutely delicious!

Of course, those handy pockets that form in the oven are also ready to be stuffed. Each flatbread makes a perfectly portioned sandwich, so you can slice it in half, gently pry open the pocket, fill it, and go. One of my favorite lunches at the moment is chicken shawarma, so I like to make extra and keep the leftovers in the fridge. Then I can stuff a pita with the chicken, some salad, and a spoonful of tzatziki sauce for lunch. The filling options are pretty much endless, so I’ve shared even more sandwich ideas below!

Homemade Pita Bread

 Cost $0.94 recipe / $0.12 serving

Make your own Homemade Pita Bread with this fun, simple recipe. This bread can be used for sandwiches, wraps, salads, or even baked into chips!

  • Gather and measure all ingredients.

  • In a small bowl, combine the warm water, sugar, and yeast. Stir to dissolve and let sit for 5 minutes or until a foam develops on top. Once a foam develops on top, add the olive oil.

  • In a large bowl, combine 1 cup of flour (half whole wheat in my case) and the salt. Stir them so they are evenly combined. Add the yeast mixture to the bowl with the flour. Stir to combine.

  • Continue mixing in flour until it forms a loose ball that you can no longer stir with a spoon. Turn the ball of dough out onto a floured surface and continue to knead in more flour until a soft and pliable (but not sticky) ball forms. You should have used around 3 cups of flour total and kneaded the dough for at least 3 minutes.

  • Place the ball of dough in an oiled bowl, cover loosely and let sit to rise for one hour or until doubled in size.

  • Punch down the risen dough and turn it out onto a floured surface. Stretch the dough into a log and cut it into 8 (or 6) equal sized pieces. Shape each piece of dough into a smooth ball and then roll it out into a flat, 6 inch diameter circle.

  • Preheat the oven to 500℉ and let the dough circles rest as the oven comes up to temp. Place a damp cloth over the dough circles so they do not dry out.

  • When the oven is hot enough, place the dough circles on a wire rack (a couple at a time) and place the rack in the oven. Watch the circles puff up as they bake. When the circle has completely inflated but not yet turned brown, you can remove it from the oven and put in the next batch. If you let them cook until golden brown, they will be crispier and may retain the inflated shape as they cool.

  • As you remove the pitas from the oven, stack them on a plate and cover with a damp cloth. The trapped steam will soften them as they cool. Serve and enjoy!

How to Make Homemade Pita Bread Step-by-Step Photos

Gather all of your ingredients.

Activate the yeast: Combine 1⅛ cups warm water, 1½ tsp sugar, and 1½ tsp instant or dry active yeast in a small bowl. Stir until dissolved, then let it sit for about 5 minutes, or until a layer of foam and small bubbles forms on top.

Now stir in 1 Tbsp olive oil.

Start the dough: Add ½ cup whole wheat flour, ½ cup all-purpose flour, and 1 tsp salt to a large bowl and stir to combine.

Pour the yeast mixture into the bowl of dry ingredients and stir gently to combine.

Add the remaining flour and knead: Gradually mix in the remaining 2 cups all-purpose flour until the dough forms a loose ball and becomes too stiff to stir.

Turn it onto a lightly floured surface and knead for at least 3 minutes, adding only enough flour to stop it from sticking. The finished dough should feel soft, smooth, and pliable, not dry or stiff. You’ll use around 3 cups of flour total, but you may need slightly more or less depending on your dough.

Let the dough rise: Place the dough in a lightly oiled bowl, cover loosely, and leave it in a warm, draft-free spot for about 1 hour, or until doubled in size. If your kitchen is chilly, place it in a turned-off oven with the light switched on.

Divide and roll the dough: Once doubled in size, gently punch down the risen dough, then transfer it to a lightly floured surface.

Now shape the dough into a log, as shown here.

Slice the log into 8 equal pieces. Form each piece into a smooth ball, then roll it into an even 6-inch circle. Keep the surface smooth and free from tears so the steam stays trapped inside as it bakes.

Rest and bake: Preheat the oven to 500ºF and cover the rolled dough with a damp cloth while the oven heats so the edges don’t dry out. Once the oven is fully preheated, carefully arrange a couple of rounds at a time on a plain metal, oven-safe wire rack.

Bake until each round has completely puffed up but is still pale. Leaving them to bake until golden brown will make them drier and crispier.

Cover and cool: Transfer each baked pita to a plate, stack them, and cover with a damp cloth. The trapped steam softens the bread as it cools, leaving it tender and lightly chewy. Repeat with the remaining dough.

Serve and enjoy!

Turn it Into a Sandwich!

Stuffing pita bread and enjoying it like a sandwich is one of my favorite ways to use this flatbread. It’s great for meal prep and easy lunches because you can fill each pocket with whatever you’ve already prepped, leftovers you need to use up, or a few simple sandwich staples. Here are some of my favorite ideas:

  • Quinoa tabbouleh with leftover grilled chicken (as shown in the photo below!) This one is super filling, seriously protein-packed, and leans into fresh Mediterranean flavors.
  • Homemade falafel with lettuce, tomato, cucumber, and tahini sauce for a hearty plant-based filling.
  • Deli ham and cheese, toasted until the bread is warm and the cheese is gooey.
  • Traditional chicken salad with crisp lettuce or sliced cucumber.
  • Like a pizza pocket with pepperoni, pizza sauce, mozzarella, and any extra toppings you have on hand.
  • Scrambled eggs, cheese, and leftover roasted veggies for an easy breakfast sandwich.
  • Curry chickpea salad with fresh spinach or crunchy cucumber for a creamy meatless filling.

Storage & Reheating

Once the bread is completely cool, store it in an airtight container or zip-top bag at room temperature for up to 2 days. Make sure the bag is sealed well so they stay soft and don’t dry out.

Refrigerator

For longer storage, keep the cooled pitas in an airtight container or zip-top bag in the refrigerator for up to 4 days.

Reheating

These are best served at room temperature or warm. To reheat, warm them briefly in the microwave, oven, or a dry skillet until soft and warmed through.

Freezer

Place parchment paper between each cooled flatbread, then transfer them to a freezer-safe bag and freeze for up to 3 months. Thaw at room temperature, then reheat using one of the methods above until soft and warm.
